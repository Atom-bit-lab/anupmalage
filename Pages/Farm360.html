Context

What is Farm360

Modern agriculture increasingly relies on data to make better decisions. Among the most important sources of that data is the soil itself its composition, nutrient levels, and overall health can influence what should be grown and how the land should be treated. For agricultural enterprises, however, the challenge becomes more complex as the number of farms, tests, samples, and farmers increases. Collecting soil data is only the first step; the real value comes from making that data understandable and actionable at scale.

Krishitantra built Farm360 to address this gap. Farm360 is an enterprise platform that helps agricultural businesses manage the complete soil-intelligence experience from creating and tracking soil tests to analyzing results, managing farms and fields, generating crop and fertilizer recommendations, and producing reports for farmers.

Problem

Growing complexity was creating friction across the product, users, and business

As Farm360 grew, its design evolved alongside the product rather than through a unified system. This created inconsistencies across the experience and made the product harder to navigate, maintain, and extend. The challenge was to bring coherence to the existing product while establishing a scalable design foundation for what came next.

Approach

Not to reinvent the whole wheel again

By the time I worked on Farm360, the platform was already being used by a growing number of enterprises. The product had proven its value, but its design had evolved alongside the business rather than from a single, unified system.

1. Diagnosis

I started by auditing Farm360 from the outside in first understanding the existing system and its visual language, then evaluating how usable the experience was. I began with a UI audit, looking at the consistency of the visual and interaction foundations across the platform. I then conducted a UX audit using established usability heuristics to identify issues in how the product was structured and experienced. Finally, conversations with business stakeholders helped connect these product-level findings to the broader business needs.

2. Discovery

The audit revealed that the issues were interconnected, not isolated. Across the platform, inconsistencies in the visual system, information architecture, navigation, interaction feedback, and reusable components were creating friction for both users and the teams building the product.

Visual inconsistency
Inconsistent typography, spacing, colours, and interaction patterns made the interface feel fragmented and reduced predictability across the product.

  • User impact: Harder to build familiarity and understand patterns consistently.
  • Business impact: Increased inconsistency as new features were introduced.

Complexity in IA & navigation
The information architecture and navigation made it harder to understand where things belonged and how different parts of the product were connected.

  • User impact: Higher cognitive load and a steeper learning curve for new users.
  • Business impact: More effort required to learn and support the platform.

Missing system feedback
Important system states and feedback were missing or unclear, leaving users uncertain about whether their actions had been completed successfully.

  • User impact: Uncertainty and repeated actions.
  • Business impact: Repeated submissions and avoidable support requests.

No centralized design foundation
Similar UI patterns were being solved independently across the product, without a centralized component library or shared design foundation.

  • Team impact: Repeated design and development effort.
  • Business impact: New features took longer to deliver and introduced further inconsistencies.

3. Reframe

"The product wasn't broken. The experience around it was.”
Farm360 already had the functionality required to support its core business — from creating soil tests and analyzing results to generating recommendations and reports. The audit didn't point to a fundamental gap in what the product could do. Instead, the friction was in how that functionality was structured, communicated, and experienced.

Thus Farm360 didn't need to reinvent its functionality. It needed a stronger usability and design foundation around it.

Results

A more consistent product, with 35% faster feature delivery

The redesign established a scalable design system with reusable components and refined interaction patterns, contributing in feature delivery time while improving consistency and maintainability across the product.

Learning

Moving from designing screens to thinking in systems

Working on this project changed how I approach product design. I learned that creating a consistent experience isn't just about designing individual screens, but about building systems that support the product as it grows.

1. Design systems are more than component libraries
I learned how foundations, semantics, and reusable components work together to create consistency, improve maintainability, and support faster feature development.

2. Systems Thinking
Learned to view a product as a collection of interconnected systems, where components, workflows, and information architecture work together to create a cohesive user experience.
