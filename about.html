About Me “He’ll be an artist,” they said when I was a kid

And then I took engineering. Just kidding, it was probably one of the best decisions I made.

Art and technology had always fascinated me, and somewhere between the two, I found design. With a background in Computer Science and experience across enterprise platforms and mobile applications, I've come to enjoy working at the intersection of people, technology, and problem-solving.

My work spans the product design process, from understanding complex workflows and identifying usability gaps to shaping information architecture, designing interactions, and building scalable design systems. I enjoy simplifying complexity, bringing consistency to products, and finding the right balance between user needs, business goals, and technical constraints.

Beyond the screens, I'm curious about how things work, why people behave the way they do, and how thoughtful design can make everyday experiences better. There's still a lot to explore, experiment with, and learn, and that's probably my favourite part of being a designer.

A little about how I think about design Lagom - “Not too little, not too much, just right”

I've always been drawn to the idea of finding the right balance. The Swedish concept of *Lagom* captures this well: not too little, not too much, but just enough.

I see the same principle in design. It's not about adding more features, stripping everything back, or following trends. It's about understanding what people need, what a product should do, and what genuinely deserves to be there.

This curiosity extends beyond how things look or function. I'm interested in how people think, form mental models, and interact with products in everyday life. Even the smallest design decisions can reduce effort, build confidence, or create delight.

I'm also drawn to the relationship between art and design. While art offers room for creative expression, design gives it purpose through problems and the people it serves. I believe the two can coexist, bringing creativity and logic together. For me, intentional design is about finding that balance between function and expression, simplicity and capability, and how a product works and makes people feel. These ideas continue to evolve as I learn and design.
